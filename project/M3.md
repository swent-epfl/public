# Project Milestone M3

_Last update: November 13, 2026_

Your project has three milestones (see the [project README](./README.md)), and this handout concerns the third and last milestone, named M3.
It covers the remaining Sprints of the semester, starting with S6.

The milestone ends on Friday, December 18. However, there are two staggered deadlines:

- The **deliverables in this document** are due Friday **December 11** at 08:45
  - Sprint S10 is your Release Sprint, in which you polish your app for real users and the BOTA. Your coaches will continue tracking your progress and giving you feedback after the above deadline. There will, however, _not_ be a Sprint Review meeting with the coaches at the end of S10.
- The **survey**, linked from the [README](../README.md), is due Friday **December 18** at 23:59. The survey is not graded but must be completed&mdash;it is an integral part of the M3 deliverables, so make sure you complete it before the deadline, or else late penalties will apply.

## Overview
M3 is the culmination of your hands-on journey in SwEnt, in which you transform your initial concept into a refined app ready for real users.
This milestone carries the most weight among the three, as it evaluates not only the progress of the last four Sprints but also the final output of your entire course project. 
By now, you should have a solid command of Scrum methodology and collaborative teamwork. 
M3 is the final stretch, where you apply your skills to deliver a high-quality, user-ready application.

>[!NOTE]
> The Battle of the Apps (BOTA) is an optional competition in which your team presents their app to an audience consisting of your peers and outside visitors. 
You can find all the details [here](BOTA.md).

Continue soliciting informal, ungraded feedback from your coaches every week on how things are going. 
You can do this both during the weekly meetings and outside the formal meetings.

## M3 Deliverables

Here is a list of items that your _team_ will need to prepare for M3.
Most should already be familiar from the deliverables for [M1](M1.md) and [M2](M2.md).

1. Updated Figma and architecture diagram driving development ➡︎ _figma.com_, named "M3" and linked from your project's _GitHub repo_
2. Up-to-date documentation of your app, written for both human developers and AI agents ➡︎ _GitHub repo_ and _GitHub wiki_ 
   - a README with an overview of the architecture and key components, setup instructions, and contribution guidelines
   - an `AGENTS.md` defining the coding standards, architectural conventions, and the build and test commands
   - everything a newcomer, human or agent, would need to know about your code repo in order to contribute effectively (explanations for hard-to-understand parts of the code, explanations for unusual design choices, team conventions, agentic workflows, processes and standards, etc.).
3. Code repo with CI and Sonar integration ➡︎ _GitHub_
   - fully functional CI pipeline
   - code analyzed by Sonar and no remaining quality issues
   - high-quality testing, including at least 4 meaningful end-to-end tests (counting the ones from M1 and M2)
4. High-quality APK generated by the CI, featuring at least two meaningful epics, ready to run on an Android smartphone ➡︎ _Releases_
5. Implementation ➡︎ _GitHub repo_
   - offline mode is fully implemented
   - supports multiple users and authentication
   - uses ≥1 public cloud service (e.g., Firebase) in addition to authentication
   - uses meaningfully ≥1 phone sensor (e.g., GPS, accelerometer, camera, mic, ...)

Additionally, each _individual_ team member should prepare the following:

1. Code: ≥2 significant PRs in each Sprint ready for review ≥24h before the end of the Sprint and merged into `main` before the end of the Sprint ➡︎ _GitHub_
2. Reviews: ≥2 significant PRs reviewed in each Sprint approved in the version merged into `main` no later than the Sprint deadline ➡︎ _GitHub_
3. Submit the survey latest by the deadline indicated above ➡︎ _Moodle_

What counts as a significant PR or a significant review, the timing expectations, and the ownership rules are the same as in [M1](M1.md) and [M2](M2.md).
The expected quality is higher than in M2, and you should continue delivering significant value in every Sprint.
Coaches will discuss your ownership of code and reviews, whether written by you or an AI.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Unlike in M2, ownership in M3 will take on a broader meaning: We will be evaluating not only how well each team member owns their own PRs and reviews but also how well each member of the team knows their app, the design, its dependencies, etc.

## Grading

In M3 we will use the belt system described in the [course README](../README.md#grading).

You earn a belt in each grade category. These category belts determine an overall belt, separately for your team grade and your individual grade, and each grade cannot exceed the range of its overall belt.

| Overall belt | Requirements |
|---|---|
| ⚪ White  | This is where you start; the only requirement is to show up |
| 🟡 Yellow | Yellow or higher in all but 2 categories |
| 🟢 Green  | Green or higher in all but 1 category, and Yellow in the remaining one |
| 🔵 Blue   | Blue or higher in all but 1 category, and Green or higher in the remaining one |
| ⚫ Black  | Black in all but 1 category, and Blue or higher in the remaining one |

<sup>1</sup>
At the beginning of the course, we set the expectation that your app makes effective use of public cloud services, seamlessly transitions between online and offline mode, and makes meaningful use of the sensors. Feature-wise, it should have at least two meaningful epics implemented. We will evaluate if you have developed both epics and if their depth and complexity is in line with expectations. We suggest that you revisit with your coaches what constitutes a meaningful epic and whether you are on track to meet expectations by the end of this milestone. The assessment will be primarily based on the APK, which is what users get.

<sup>2</sup>
Your app should work end-to-end seamlessly and coherently. Users should be able to create accounts, interact with the app and other users in natural and predictable ways, store and retrieve their data/information, be able to switch from one device to another, etc. The provided functionality should work reliably and survive the stress imposed by clueless users.

<sup>3</sup>
To be maintainable, your code must be modular and consistent in its conventions. Keep each function concise and focused on a single responsibility, avoid oversized classes and methods, and choose descriptive names throughout your code.
Your code must also be well tested. High coverage matters, and so does the quality of your tests.
We will pay close attention to the quality of your code.
Regardless of who/what wrote the code and tests, you own them.

<sup>4</sup>
As a final release to put in the hands of real users, your app must have great usability. Ensure that the app is intuitive, allowing even first-time users to navigate easily without assistance. Focus on providing a streamlined experience that promotes seamless interaction with the app.

<sup>5</sup>
As in every Sprint Review, your team should prepare and run a demo. The demo should be clear and structured. It should show the real state of the app and highlight this Sprint's Increment.

<sup>6</sup>
Your project should have comprehensive documentation across all levels of the app written for both human developers and AI agents. 
This includes an organized README that provides an overview of the app’s architecture and key components. The README should serve as a central guide, including resources like the Figma and an architecture diagram, both of which should be up-to-date and driving development. 
It also includes an `AGENTS.md` (and any rules, review checklists, or skills it references) that states your coding standards, architectural boundaries, how to build and test the app, and the workflow that agents are expected to follow.
This documentation approach ensures that new contributors can quickly understand the app's structure and design, making onboarding and future development smoother.

<sup>7</sup>
You own every line you merge. Beyond that, you are responsible for understanding the structure of the codebase and the architecture of the app: how its components interact, and how the code you merge fits into the rest of the app.

Remember that many of the items above were explained in the deliverables for [M1](M1.md) and [M2](M2.md), so do revisit them if anything is unclear.
